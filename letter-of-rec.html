Jon E. Thai's Letter of Recommendation Instructions

Below is a checklist of things I ask for when I write a letter for students. (Some of these you may already have done, and some may not be appropriate for you; just do what makes the most sense or what we may have discussed in person).

  1. Please try to let me know at least a month in advance. (I will still try to accommodate shorter deadlines, but the letter is likely to be better if I have more time.)
  2. A list of links to the instructions/prompt for the letter writer, with deadlines.
  3. Instructions on how to submit. (e.g. email to XXX@email.com, “you will receive an email from XXX, or send me a link, etc.)
  4. Please also find out things like whether I need to mail a physical letter vs. upload to a website, etc. Please also let me know if my letter has a different deadline than the rest of your application, or if there are any other special instructions I should be aware of as your letter writer.
  5. I have a strong preference for PDF documents. A spreadsheet is also great if you have one to organize all of the necessary information.
  6. Please include your name in the filename of any documents that you send me.
  7. Let me know who are your other letter writers. Include a sentence of context for them as well (e.g. advised my honors thesis) as this helps me better understand the context and perspective that I may help add to your application(s).
  8. Once you have drafts of anything you're submitting, please send them to me. The basic principle is that I can't write about something I don't know about and this helps provide context on your application.
  9. If you are an undergraduate, I'd prefer you send an unofficial copy of your transcript too.

Finally, on the application page, please use the following for my personal information.

Name: Jonathan E. Thai

Affiliation: The University of Scranton

Department: Chemistry

Position/title: Assistant Professor

Phone: (570) 941-5971

Email: Jonathan.thai@scranton.edu

If possible, please do send me a frequent email (every week or so, potentially more as the deadline grows closer). I have not missed a deadline before, but this just helps keep it at the top of my to-do list. At some point, I will send you an email back saying that I have submitted your letter. If you do not receive confirmation from me, then you can assume that I have not submitted your letter yet and you should continue to pester me and not feel bad doing so.

Best of luck,

JET
